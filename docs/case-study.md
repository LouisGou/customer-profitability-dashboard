# Nova: growth, retention, and acquisition tradeoffs

This case study uses a fictional subscription business. It demonstrates how to interpret the dashboard; it is not evidence of a real business outcome.

## Observed results: January–December 2025

- 570 customers acquired; 391 paying in December.
- $222,726.40 revenue and $166,747.26 gross profit (74.9% gross margin).
- $88,656 acquisition spending; $78,091.26 contribution after acquisition spending.
- 349 of 570 customers recovered acquisition costs. The recovered-only median payback was 2 months, which must be considered together with the 221 unrecovered customers.

| Channel      | Average CAC | M6 retention | Eligible customers | Observed contribution |
| ------------ | ----------: | -----------: | -----------------: | --------------------: |
| Organic      |      $85.77 |        67.6% |                 68 |            $44,603.77 |
| Paid search  |     $288.58 |        39.1% |                 69 |           -$16,525.58 |
| Partnerships |     $153.35 |        67.6% |                 37 |            $14,160.80 |
| Referral     |      $55.39 |        80.8% |                 52 |            $35,852.27 |

## Decision to investigate

Paid search generated $55,451.40 revenue, but its acquisition spending exceeded its observed gross profit. Before increasing that budget, investigate targeting, conversion costs, customer fit, and retention by signup month. Referral combines a lower observed acquisition cost with higher M6 retention in this sample; test whether it can scale without degrading either metric.

## Limits of the conclusion

Channel comparisons are observational. Customers differ in plan, signup date, and available follow-up time. The data cannot establish that a channel caused better retention. Contribution excludes overhead and taxes; newer customers may recover acquisition spending in future months. The dataset does not estimate lifetime value, future retention, or causal returns on increased marketing spending.
